# Motion Library / 动效库

Every scene module is one reusable motion. Pick motions by the user's theme, not by copying any source reel: choose a tone profile, a carrier element and palette, then fill each narrative slot with a motion whose role and tone fit. Sources only tell you where a motion was learned.
每个场景模块就是一个可复用动效。按用户主题挑选动效，不复刻任何参考片：先定调性、贯穿元素和配色，再为每个叙事位置挑选角色与调性都匹配的动效。

Sources / 来源：**S** = original showreel (Motion Design Showreel). **R1** = dot reel (reference 1, red dot). **R2** = chapter reel (reference 2, orange ball, chapter HUD). **R3a** = fast tech reel (reference 3 part 1: CRT HUD, glitch, pillars, recap mosaic). **R3b** = Swiss portfolio pages with liquid-chrome objects (reference 3 part 2). **R4** = ignition showreel (reference 4: comet ignition, beat-hit words on colour fields, colour matrix, fluid blob, bursting sphere, UI cards, glitch-to-arc lockup, REC HUD). **P** = product scene built for launches.

## 1. Catalog / 动效目录

Roles: `open` opener · `promise` headline / one-liner · `point` single selling point or concept word · `list` several points · `product` product image · `proof` real numbers · `mood` emotional / brand statement · `peak` energy peak · `close` logo lockup.
Tone tags: `calm` `crafted` `tech` `energetic` `playful` `premium`.
Exit: `self` = the scene finishes its own hand-off (burst fill, collapse to a point, disperse, dolly + smear, sweep, whip), so the next cut is automatically a hard cut. `trans` = uses the style transition.

## 2. Matching procedure / 匹配流程

1. **Tone profile** from the theme (pick 1–2 tags): developer/AI/hardware/fintech → `tech`; design tools, studios, culture → `crafted`; luxury, beauty, finance → `premium` + `calm`; sports, gaming, launches → `energetic`; food, kids, pets → `playful`; wellness, education → `calm`.
2. **Carrier element**: one element that travels through the film (dot, ball, energy orb). Set `carrier` to the palette role holding the brand's hottest colour. Prefer `self`-exit motions next to each other so the carrier keeps hands off between shots; mixing with `trans` modules is allowed (they use the style transition).
3. **Narrative slots** (15 s ≈ 8 slots at 128 BPM × 4 beats, or 120 BPM with 3–5 beats): open → promise → 2–4 × point/list/product → proof (only with real data) → mood or peak → close. For each slot pick a module whose role fits and whose tone overlaps the profile.
4. **Variety rules**: no module twice in a 15 s film (`split_hero` may repeat with different objects); do not put three 3D/particle modules (`grid_wave3d` `point_ring` `sphere3d` `particle_word` `depth_tunnel` `pillar_wave` `infinity_particles` `sphere_burst`) in a row; `grid_recap` only after ≥3 scenes, right before the close; alternate `bg` value between neighbours; put one peak in the last third before the close.
5. **Do not reproduce a source reel**: never use all eight modules of one source in their original order unless the user asks for that preset. Mix sources and rewrite all copy for the theme.
6. **Opt-in only motions**: `depth_tunnel` 纵深隧道 and `ribbon_loop` 条纹色带 are not defaults. Never use them to fill a product slot or as a fallback; pick them only when their visual idea matches the product or brand (depth/speed/travel for the tunnel; stripes/ribbons/laces/wrapping for the ribbon), and state the fit in the plan. For a product slot default to `product_hero` or `product_glow`.
7. Optional HUD: `hud.mode: "chapter"` = `00 - NAME` chapter labels, BPM, frame counter (showreels, tech, studios); `"pages"` = `01 — 06` page counter + progress line (Swiss portfolio, editorial, music); `"tech"` = CRT-style readouts; `"reel"` = corner brackets, `NN - NAME` chapter label, running timecode, scrub ruler, resolution/fps and blinking REC (showreels, studios, launches). Transition `scan_glitch` (set on the incoming scene) = blue flash with RGB-split stretched name and scanlines; use once, into the close. `decode_lockup` lists scene `name`s as chapter nav unless `nav` is set.
8. Chrome objects (`split_hero`, `object_lockup`, `beat_jelly`) take their glow/core colour from `accent` (default `a2`); an acid or neon `a2` gives the liquid-chrome look, a brand colour keeps it on-brand. Chrome is shaded per pixel (`mgfx_chrome.py`: black studio with strip lights, accent glowing from inner faces / star centre, bloom, no cast shadow); scenes with chrome objects render several times slower (≈12–15 min for 15 s at 1080p60), so tell the user the render time.

Industry picks (starting points; adapt to the brand and swap single scenes freely). `*` = needs real user data, otherwise replace it (`stat` → `kinetic_words` or `cross_ripple`; `data_dash` → `ui_cards` labelled as illustrative, or `grid_ripple`). `[img]` = needs a product image. `[chrome]` = liquid-chrome scenes, slow render.

## 2b. Presenting recommendations / 向用户呈现推荐

At intake (SKILL Step 2), once the theme is known, present motion recommendations in the user's language. Never show code names (`ball_drop`) to the user; use the 中文名 column (or a plain English name), unless the user asks for module names.

1. One sentence of diagnosis: the industry row(s) and tone you read from the brief, and why.
2. **2–3 options**, each a different direction: **推荐方案** (best fit, from the industry row adapted to the brand), **稳妥方案** (simpler, faster render, fewer 3D/chrome scenes), **大胆方案** (a stronger look from a neighbouring tone or a preset). Options must differ by at least half of their scenes.
3. For each option give: style and palette; pace (BPM, seconds per scene); a scene table — order, motion name, what it shows for this brand (draft copy or content), why it fits; what the user must supply (`[img]` product image, `*` real numbers, logo); estimated render time at 1080p60 (≈1–3 min; add ≈10 min if any `[chrome]` scene).
4. Ask in one question card: pick an option, or swap named scenes. Swaps are made from the same narrative role in the catalog. The chosen option becomes the scene list of the production plan (Step 3).

## 4. Format and fonts

- All library modules are tuned for ≈1.9 s (4 beats at 128 BPM); 3–6 beats at 110–140 BPM work. Minimum 1.0 s.
- Works in 16:9, 1:1, 4:5, 9:16.
- `style`: `swiss` suits most library modules (no shake); `punch` adds harder cuts.
- Page modules (R3b) read best at 2.2–2.8 s (4–5 beats at 108–116 BPM).
- Font kinds: `display` heavy sans, `mono`, `serif` (Didot/Bodoni; Chinese Songti), `serif_it` italic serif (used by `serif_flow`).

## R5 chain: idea-to-form (designed, not module-swapped)
`prompt_type` → `frame_trace` → `media_morph` → `canvas_zoom` → `caret_lockup` is ONE continuous gesture: caret → stroke → frame → media → shared canvas → caret writes the wordmark. Each scene ends in the exact geometry the next starts from, so the hard cuts are invisible. Use the whole chain for creative / AI / collaboration platforms (example: `films/minimax-design`), or lift single links (e.g. `prompt_type` → any frame-based scene). Lesson: when a film feels monotonous, design one carrier and its causal hand-offs first, then choose or write modules — don't just shuffle modules.
R5 options: works on light or dark backgrounds (frames/tiles become white cards on light themes). Top-level `"frame_shape": "icon"` draws frames as squares with one rounded bottom-left corner and dot handles on the other three (the MiniMax Design icon); `"round"` = rounded rect with square handles. With a layered logo, `canvas_zoom` collapses every tile into the logo's icon square, and `caret_lockup` lets the mascot peek in before the caret reveals the wordmark. Layer geometry constants (`LOGO_*`, `WM_X/Y`, `SQ0/SQ1`) are in `mgfx_ideaform.py`; adjust them for another layered logo. Spec `fonts` paths may be relative to the spec file.

## R7 chain: reelcraft showcase (hand-tuned reel rhythm)
`line_split` → `marquee_swap` (`transition: iris`) → `orbit_morph` (`transition: blinds`) → `style_cuts` → `converge_lockup`. Ported from a hand-tuned 8 s showcase (120 BPM, `min_scene` 1.25). Extras for type variety: `split_flap`, `slice_type`, `outline_echo` — mix into any chain. (A letter-shuffle module was tried and dropped: scrambled letters read as noise, not motion — keep letters on their reading line.) Rhythm lessons that made it feel natural: ~1.25–2 s per scene; each scene is burst → hold → fast exit; letters rise out of a mask with overshoot (never just fade); something always moves in the background; hand-offs are match cuts (iris opens from a point of the outgoing scene, the last shape fills the screen in the next colour); shake + RGB on beats, grain + vignette throughout (`fx`). Don't compress modules under ~1 s to fit more in — cut modules instead; speeding old scenes up is not a new design.

## R6 chain: type-led creative platform
`type_prompt` → `pixel_word` → `media_type` → `select_words` → `caret_lockup` (layered logo). Same brand logic as R5 but every beat is carried by big type — use it when the brief is kinetic typography (this skill's default promise). R5 is the graphic / UI-led alternative. Example: `films/minimax-design` v3.
