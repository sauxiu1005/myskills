# General Style and Story Identity

## General style: portable across stories

Earth-toned two-dimensional hand drawing, uneven thick brush lines, restrained flat fills and slight paper texture; large heads, small torsos, thin black limbs, solid palms/feet with simple fingers/toes and hide materials. No realistic skin, plastic 3D or bare line-end hands and feet. This language does not prescribe one face or costume.

Keep whites and small pupils independent; upper/lower lids move separately from brushstroke brows. A small oval nose is legible. Open mouths have a black cavity; corners can rise, drop, tighten or slacken. A few front teeth and a tongue appear when the pose warrants them, not in every expression. These are available acting parts, not fixed emotions.

## Story identity: design each character separately

Faces may be round, long, angular or asymmetric; brow shape and eye spacing serve role, personality and relationships. Hair may be tidy, tied, short, sparse or messy. Stubble and beards may be absent. Messy hair and stubble are not universal style requirements or acceptance criteria.

Hide shoulder construction, length, edges, patterns, belts and accessories follow each role's actions and story. Props follow plot needs, including no handheld prop. Do not copy an old character's bone accessory, spear, torch or axe by default. Once chosen, facial silhouette, hair, costume and distinctive marks remain consistent within that story.

## Expressions and preview layout: choose from the plot

List the character's emotional changes and key actions, then select the most useful preview views. Curiosity to realization, pride to embarrassment, or composure to alarm are optional examples, never a required emotion set.

Full-body, half-body, profile, hand/prop or face views serve plot and reference needs. Layout, count and ratio follow the current deliverable, not a fixed grid. Add an expression sheet only when requested. Depict full hands and feet in full-body views; crop portraits as composed.

## Optional references versus current-story anchors

No external image is needed to design a preview from the story and style. Only user-designated external references or old characters enter the inputs. Identify whether they donate style, identity or pose; style is not automatic identity reuse. Exclude captions, watermarks and player artifacts.

After video confirmation, generated character images anchor this story and flow directly into video without automated quality inspection. Wait only for explicitly requested user image review. New stories do not inherit identity unless requested. Never store fixed paths, nodes or calibration maps in general rules.
