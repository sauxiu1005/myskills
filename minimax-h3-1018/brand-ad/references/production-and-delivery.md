# Production and delivery

Continue from the scope, decisions and authorization the user has already given. Present unresolved material choices, proposed copy/sound, deliverables and known cost together in one production proposal; do not add per-image approvals or a new review chain. Changes to message, identity, deliverables or spend follow that same cadence.

## Ready inputs and capabilities

Reuse supplied or previously prepared work before proposing generation. When images are necessary, finish only the missing coverage, then develop the actual video prompt from the usable inputs. Existing storyboards and images need not pass through our templates. Ordinary camera and cut refinements within the accepted idea do not reopen the creative decision.

Carry consistency anchors and scene anchors with distinct roles. Consistency sources preserve people/product identity and required views. Scene anchors establish environment, composition, lighting, atmosphere and changing states without product-appearance, logo or text consistency requirements; any illustrative subject is not an identity source. Mix models by role, using scene-design strengths for environments and identity-preserving strengths for subject anchors. In the video prompt, combine accurate subjects with the chosen scene design using supported references; do not require an extra complete advertising still between image preparation and video. Resolve only actual reference conflicts within the agreed scope. A first scene exploration of 3–4 states is not a quota for consistency anchors or video segments; use the states that actually contribute to the film.

Check the chosen model's current durations, format, reference combinations, native audio and typography capabilities. Honor a capable user-selected model. A video model that supports joint references, creative type and native sound can suit an integrated production route; feasibility comes from current capability, not a cached model assumption. Capability facts must not replace or shorten the creative prompt.

## Generation units and continuity

Design the film before splitting it. Distinguish exact duration, upper bound and suggested length. Keep one supported audiovisual unit when it can carry the film; otherwise choose complete actions and useful cut boundaries, not equal batches. Each unit needs its order, local progression, actual inputs and required preceding state. Later units continue the film without repeating its introduction and brand close. Ready independent units can proceed together; real dependencies wait for their inputs.

Carry identity and essential world facts across units while allowing varied composition. Describe outgoing/incoming action phase, direction, visual relationship and sound state. Prior frames or motion references are used only when supported and actually needed; ordinary references do not silently become first/last-frame bindings.

Keep exact narration and consistent vocal character, with complete phrase boundaries and no duplicate voice track. Native music belongs in the prompt when planned. Text saying “continue the music” does not ensure a continuous performance across independent generations: use a supported single unit, shared audio reference or planned common music bed, or design a deliberate sound boundary and disclose the limitation. When a shared bed is planned, avoid duplicate generated music while retaining voice/Foley/ambience.

## Delivery and revision

Keep the full generation prompts and real returned files. Assemble in planned order, not task completion order; use only planned cuts, handles and sound work. Brand names, logos and creative copy are not added or repaired by later overlays.

Distinguish confirmed file facts, actually observed quality and user acceptance. Assess identity, message, composition, motion, rhythm, copy and sound only within available observations and the authorized review scope; report unverified aspects honestly. Do not infer quality from a completed generation or storyboard appearance. Preserve completed outputs and continue unaffected work when a dependency is missing.

Report actual duration/format and specific limitations. Duration differences or aesthetic concerns do not authorize automatic regeneration, trimming, padding, looping or retiming. User-requested revisions affect the stated scope only. If it is unclear whether a generation already started, confirm that before generating again to avoid duplicate work.
