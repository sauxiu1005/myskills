# Advertising idea and audiovisual prompt

Use this method for the current creative gap. Supplied scripts, boards and accepted treatments remain the basis; do not start a second ideation exercise when the idea is already established. Image preparation uses [tvc-moodboard-creative-guide.md](tvc-moodboard-creative-guide.md) only for missing coverage; execution uses [production-and-delivery.md](production-and-delivery.md).

## Turn the idea into a visible experience

Connect what the audience should remember to a sensory association, use consequence, surprising relationship, transformation, performance or graphic event. Product views need a meaningful relationship, not just a list of required angles. A direct demonstration can be as deliberate as a delayed reveal; neither is compulsory.

When creating a new idea, consider distinct expressions internally and choose one with a specific payoff. A short brief permits developing photography, staging, effects, rhythm and sound, not inventing product facts or marks. Preserve supplied wording. Keep the creative card compact: takeaway, idea/world, key events/rhythm, mandatory requirements and remaining preparation. Existing work can already contain all of this.

User requirements and verified identity stay fixed. Proposed camera angles, pacing, color distribution and sound remain creative choices that can improve within the accepted direction. Do not convert every incidental choice into a new prohibition or review form.

## Rhythm follows attention

A visual event, shot, generation unit and storyboard panel are different things. Shape what catches attention, develops, changes meaning and lingers, then allocate time for the actual duration, complete action, speech and reading.

- Associative montage: tactile details, gestures, reactions or environment fragments accumulate feeling and return to a recognizable subject or consequence. Emotional buildup may use denser cuts.
- Continuous discovery: travel along or through the world while scale, focus, access or speed reveals new information. A long take needs internal development.
- Action across viewpoints: cut within an understandable action, preserving direction and momentum while omitting routine intervals.
- Return with changed meaning: revisit a gesture, shape or place after events alter its significance.

Choose or combine these when useful. Cut density, motion speed and information density are separate controls. A dense passage may open into a broad reveal; a quiet moment can intensify through small discoveries. No fixed shot count, seconds-per-shot rule, transition quota or ending percentage. A supplied board does not imply equal panel timing. Longer films develop consequences instead of repeating miniature ad openings and endings.

## Write photography and transformation concretely

Give each important image a focal hierarchy, viewpoint, scale and foreground/subject/background relationship. Choose negative space, occlusion, depth corridors or scale contrast for a reason. Light should reveal the actual glass thickness, metal texture, liquid tension, cloth or paper response. Suspended space does not require a ground plane; shadows and reflections follow the selected world.

Separate camera, subject and environment motion. A slowly rotating product can support fast smooth camera travel. A fixed camera can witness an energetic material event. Premium does not imply slow, silent, low-saturation or empty. Describe a selected move as **starting view → path and speed development → reveal → landing**. A near-surface skim can accelerate then open into a distant hero; name the changing view rather than arbitrary coordinates.

Give effects a source, behavior and resolution: fluid stretches and breaks, particles follow a curve then disperse, geometry unfolds to expose an interior. Effects need no separate trigger. Identity consistency does not prohibit camera roll, optical bridges, transformation or speed contrast when they fit the concept and preserve required product structure.

At a meaningful cut specify the outgoing cue and incoming focal landing. Choose what the film needs:

| Mechanism | What the prompt makes explicit |
| --- | --- |
| Shape/composition match | The contour, position or negative space inherited by the next subject |
| Action match | The movement phase, direction and momentum carried across views |
| Foreground occlusion | The existing material covering the image and the new scene it clears into |
| Optical/material bridge | The motivated reflection, refraction, light or liquid filling the view and its resolution |
| Contrast/hard cut | A decisive change of scale, energy, space or meaning with a clear new focus |
| Sound bridge | The voice, action or ambience that links or anticipates the next image |

These are options, not mandatory transitions. Direct cuts or uninterrupted action may be strongest. Avoid generic ban lists that cancel the chosen treatment.

## Sound and copy develop with the picture

Choose native music, voice, ambience, Foley or deliberate silence for the concept. No separate music file does not mean no music. Describe character, entry, buildup, accents, ending and where music yields to a line or action. Not every cut needs a whoosh.

Narration uses exact proposed words, supported claims, language, vocal character, delivery and picture relationship. An emotional line need not invent a performance specification. Key typography highlights meaning rather than transcribing every spoken sentence; use [voice-keyword-typography.md](voice-keyword-typography.md) for letterform, spatial relation and readable entrance/exit. Preserve user no-text/no-voice choices. Across independently generated units, follow the actual continuity plan rather than promising identical music from prose.

## Compile the full local progression

Once the necessary real inputs are available, develop the shot treatment against them. The brief or card is not a ceiling on detail. Write enough for a reader to picture what changes, how it is photographed and how it sounds; “premium macro → reveal → hero” is insufficient. Expand events rather than repeating constraints.

Use the following scaffold, omitting irrelevant fields rather than submitting empty labels:

```text
Create {duration and format} ad for {subject}, expressing {specific idea}.
Visual world: {space, light/color relationships, art direction, material behavior}.
References: {actual input order, identity/look/state contributions and transfer limits}.
Preserve {verified identity, supplied text and mandatory requirements}.
Editorial intent: {how attention, motion and sound develop}.

{Time window / passage}:
The viewer first sees {scene, subject state, viewpoint, scale, depth and focal read}.
{Action} causes {visible material, light, environmental or emotional response}.
The camera starts {view}, moves {path/direction/speed and optical development},
reveals {new image or relationship}, and lands {view/state}.
{Internal discoveries or cuts; outgoing cue → bridge → incoming focal subject}.
{Music, ambience, Foley or exact voice line} enters, changes or yields with {event}.
{Exact text if needed}: {letterform, hierarchy, position/depth, entrance, reading, exit}.
Leave {continuing action/visual state/sound} for the next passage.

Ending: {recognizable resolution, required identity/copy, background motion, audio tail}.
Relevant constraints: {concrete identity facts, user exclusions or specific failure risks}.
```

A passage can contain several cuts or an evolving long take. Choose an actual treatment, not unresolved “push or orbit” alternatives. Keep required actions, order, wording and identity precise; leave room for natural motion and microtiming. Do not freeze every minor cut or animate storyboard grid borders. Each generation unit repeats necessary context and its full local progression without “same as above.” Preserve this detailed prompt through submission; capability adaptation must not silently summarize it.

Before generation, read the actual prompt for a visible idea/payoff, meaningful adjacent changes, tangible composition/material, purposeful motion and rhythm, complete speech/reading, real reference roles and a coherent ending. Resolve contradictions such as banning the chosen effect or removing useful sound without a reason. This is a writing check, not authorization for extra media generation; actual quality claims require actual observations.
