# Homepage visual treatments

Read only the treatment that matches the request. These are visual treatments for a single quick brand-ad showcase, not a second planning workflow. Use [showcase-prompt-core.md](showcase-prompt-core.md) for the idea and full audiovisual prompt, and [production-and-delivery.md](production-and-delivery.md) for generation scope and delivery.

## Product hero and material reveal

Use when a product is the unmistakable protagonist. Preserve the verified silhouette, proportions, components and materials, then place the product in a designed advertising world. Choose an observable material or performance event: a surface catches and releases light, a structure opens, a fluid or particle system responds to movement, or a useful product action changes the surrounding space. The frame can move quickly and cut densely when the event supports it; premium does not mean slow, silent or empty.

Build the image around a first focal read, a path for the eye and a visible consequence. Vary scale and access to the product while returning to a recognizable identity cue. Spatial type, copy, native music, narration and contact sounds enter as part of the same visual event. Do not turn every showcase into a catalogue of identical feature close-ups.

## Brand visual process

Use when a wordmark, visual system or packaging identity develops on screen. Warm paper or off-white material, ink-black marks, restrained ginger/yellow accents, paper fibers, print texture, contact shadows and card thickness can form the world when they belong to the brief. Show a design mark becoming a coherent identity through collage, page turns, layered sheets or a resolved brand composition.

Describe the page-turn axis, stagger, thickness, overlap, changing shadows and incoming image. Protected lettering comes from the verified identity input; new creative copy uses native typography. Resolve into a readable identity with a background that can continue moving or changing light.

## Product material poetry

Use when product, packaging, ingredients, paper, labels or craft actions carry the idea. A top-down luminous acrylic or archive-like display can provide a distinct material world, but it is not a generic blank logo card. Let arrangement, touch, page turns, occlusion, light changes and matched shapes create progression. Breathing images earn their duration through a material discovery or a change in relationship.

Keep the product or package connected to its verified identity while allowing close tactile views. Avoid unrelated neon, HUD or generic spins. Coordinate paper, object and surface sounds with the selected native musical or narrative development.

## Direct brand showcase

Use for a direct branded reveal or a simple website, app, product-page or store-screen showcase. Let the actual placement determine format, readability and loop/end behavior. Let the product, service or identity move through one clear visual relationship: invitation to response, object to consequence, mark to world, or detail to hero. Use a readable brand ending when required, but keep the background alive through motion, texture or changing light.

This treatment does not invent interface content, claims or a second logo. It does not require a fixed hold length, a fixed number of shots or a separate review chain. The final prompt still states the actual copy, camera path, cuts, sound and ending.

## Future-system montage

Use when a recognizable brand anchor develops through system graphics or material transformations as the main idea. Retain a boundless black/white/gray world, contrast pulses and side/rim light when they fit the brief. Tracking marks, wireframes, scanning, glitch, glass/chip layers, metal embossing or particle reconstruction should transform the same identity and reveal new relationships; do not collect effects as a checklist or invent readable technical claims. Describe the outgoing material event, camera passage, incoming state and electronic or tactile sound cue. Product-local particles or scanning do not alone require this treatment.

## Shared guardrails

Identity and approved copy remain stable in the film. Photography, pacing, color distribution, effects and sound can develop within the chosen treatment. Consistency anchors preserve the subjects; scene anchors establish environment, lighting, composition and creative states without carrying product or text consistency requirements. Existing storyboards or selected frames can supply those roles when sufficient. Scene references do not force every video view to repeat their composition. Use only relevant references for each generation unit and report any actual duration or identity limitation.
