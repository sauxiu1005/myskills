# Product promo: five-second shots and resource-first assembly

## Scope and precedence

Integrated from the user's supplied production supplement. Apply inside the existing product-promo branch, not as a separate Skill or a new tool dependency. The source's role labels mean script writing, storyboard design, media production and final editing; actual execution uses currently available capabilities. Preserve evidence checks, finalized artwork, creative and storyboard approval, opt-in audio/text, and shot-to-detail correspondence. Updating the Skill never authorizes recreating old media.

## Timing and unresolved choices

- Each storyboard shot and its final edited allocation is 5 seconds. The full film is 15–30 seconds, including packaging, titles, transitions and ending. Offer 15 / 20 / 25 / 30 seconds, corresponding to 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 shots, when duration is unknown; these are choices, not assumed defaults.
- Use a selection card for unresolved duration, frame/orientation and audio preference, at most three questions per round. Reuse confirmed choices. Record visual style, customer-facing language, model preference and any brand hold requirements; ask only when a missing item blocks production.
- A request outside 15–30 seconds, or a duration such as 18 seconds that cannot be filled by five-second shots, needs a decision: choose a compatible duration or explicitly approve a different timing scope. Never round, shorten, extend, split deliverables or override a confirmed duration silently. A longer shot needs an explicit exception, not a model-limit rationale.
- Generation length is not final shot length. Check current model limits rather than assuming the source document's generic 30-second model ceiling. With an approved feasible route, trim excess handles to the five-second shot without losing action, text readability or brand holds. If unsupported, resolve the route before spending. Validate individual shot allocations as well as actual total export duration.

## Six production steps

1. **Write the promo script.** Produce a reviewable text document with title, type, product/SKU, goal, audience, aspect ratio, total duration, visual style, consumer language and model preference. Map verified claims and authorized brand assets to Hook–Body–CTA. Reuse prior confirmed inputs; obtain creative approval before dependent production.
2. **Design the storyboard.** Define recurring key scene elements, shot list and audio layers. Each shot has a stable identifier, class, five-second in/out points, whether the product appears, source artwork/version/module, scene/set, story action, shot size, angle, movement, exact copy, layout/readable hold, transition, audio cue and brand/packaging hold. Keep this within the existing full-storyboard review, not a duplicate questionnaire.
3. **Prepare visual references for every shot.** After approval of the written storyboard, batch-produce the required storyboard stills and reusable product/scene/person/detail elements. Every shot must have its own addressable visual reference covering its intended composition and relevant elements before video production; one generic mood board is insufficient. Reuse an approved image that fully covers a shot instead of regenerating it. New planning visuals remain subordinate to finalized main/detail artwork, not replacement product facts or a Photoshop trigger. Show the completed visual shot set for approval before videos. If still generation is forbidden, reuse sufficient approved visual assets; if coverage is missing, pause for supplied assets or an explicit scope change rather than bypassing this dependency with text alone.
4. **Produce a video for each shot.** Batch-produce the approved shots, carrying each shot's visual reference from step 3 together with its bound original artwork and identity anchors. Do not alternate generating one reference and one video while other shot visuals remain unresolved. Ordinary composition references are not automatically exact opening frames. Verify actual structure, feature visibility, composition and copy; retry only failing shots.
5. **Produce the storyboard's approved audio layers.** After shot-video production, generate, import or reuse every approved music, narration, dialogue, ambience and effect layer listed in the audio plan. Omitted or declined layers remain absent: 'all audio layers' does not authorize unsolicited voices or effects. Use one owner per layer and a coherent music bed. Plan timing and rights before production; an approved lip-sync or sound-driven shot must obtain its driving audio before that dependent shot, with the exception recorded. Do not create duplicate native and replacement music or voices.
6. **Edit after resources are ready, then guide export.** Check the resource register for every approved shot and audio layer, assemble in the storyboard order, apply purposeful cuts, source-matched text and authorized branding, mix sound and review the full film. Missing required resources block final assembly, not successful unrelated work. Confirm only unresolved edit/export decisions. Export with available tools when authorized; otherwise provide accurate guidance for the actual editor and clearly state that export remains unfinished. Deliver a playable accessible file and never equate a timeline, pending render or download instructions with completed export.

## Two shot classes

- **Product in environment / hero showcase.** Use the actual SKU from approved main imagery in the same approved key-scene world. Specify product + an authorized protagonist or POV + visible action. Product-only is valid; this classification does not authorize adding a person, hand or device. Make product identity and use context legible, rather than repeating a static package on a desk.
- **Supporting / feature and atmosphere.** Use a bound product-detail close-up, texture, verified UI, environmental action or abstract motion that serves the same brief. Explicitly mark 'product absent' when it is not shown. A product-free shot inherits approved world/design anchors instead of inventing a product view; never place a component-specific label over scenery that cannot show the named component. Lifestyle examples such as crowds, skating or skydiving are optional only when relevant and approved, never mandatory templates or evidence of performance.
- Intercut the two classes when relevant to the approved brief. Identify each shot's contribution rather than imposing a fixed class ratio. Pure atmosphere does not prove deployment, durability, safety or a technical capability; any implied product advantage still needs evidence. Source-incompatible worlds or new actions require approval.

## Editing, camera grammar and branding

- Prefer concise five-second shots and purposeful hard cuts between ideas over a default long take. Every cut changes visual information clearly: scale, action, angle, detail or setting. One shot communicates one main point.
- Vary full / medium / close-up framing where justified. A useful, non-mandatory arc is establishing context → feature detail → emotion or texture → controlled product/packaging/brand climax. Never invent a Logo or extra packaging solely to fill the arc.
- Plan varied angles and motion within reference and geometry constraints: justified Dutch angle or high/low view, slight rotation, push-in, pull-out/reveal, pan/sway or arc; use whip pans sparingly. Alternate only when it serves a visible reason, not a camera-effect checklist. Keep labels, product identity and readable holds stable. Do not reveal unsupported hidden sides or break the approved source composition for energy.
- Within a shot, time action entrance, reveal and readable hold to give internal rhythm. Internal timing does not silently expand the five-second allocation or disguise extra unrelated shots. If a genuinely longer shot is required, approve that timing exception explicitly.
- Record required packaging views, authorized Logo source, screen size/position and minimum uninterrupted hold in the storyboard. Preserve them during trimming and assembly. A fade cannot consume a required readable hold; missing Logo or packaging assets require a real asset or an approved alternative, never invention.

## On-screen text and quality gates

Extract relevant, verified wording from approved detail pages; preserve composition, font appearance, size hierarchy and the product-to-text association under the existing typography contract. No gibberish, blur, accidental mixed languages or extra generated labels. Approved multilingual copy is not accidental language mixing. Original font names require evidence; substitutions require approval. Do not automatically copy unsupported claims or add text to a text-free request.

Before final export, check: every shot is covered by approved visuals; correct class and product presence; five-second allocations and approved total; clear cuts and differentiated framing; each claim matched to visible evidence; accurate source-matched text; required brand/packaging holds; all required audio present exactly once; readable, playable export. Distinguish pass, fail and unchecked. Fix failed shots or layers only; metadata cannot replace viewing/listening, and user acceptance remains required.
