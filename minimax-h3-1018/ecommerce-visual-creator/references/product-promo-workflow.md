## Adapted source workflow

# Promo Video

Create a publish-ready promotional video from verified product or brand information. The workflow moves from source and asset validation to product visual development, creative approval, storyboard review, segmented production, assembly, and delivery.

## Production Modes

- **Fast draft**: use recommended defaults, but still complete source validation, product visual preparation, creative confirmation, storyboard coverage, and capability checks before expensive production.
- **Director mode**: pause for approval after the brief, creative directions, product visuals, storyboard, video segments, and rough cut.

Automatic continuation permits recommended defaults only. It never authorizes invented claims, unverified brand use, skipped confirmations, or silently changed delivery requirements.

## Inputs

Accept product or brand descriptions, official websites or specific product pages, authorized documents, product images, packaging, interface screenshots, renders, existing footage, reference ads, visual references, confirmed claims, CTA, audience, channel, language, duration, aspect ratio, and audio preference.

A homepage is not automatically a product source. Resolve a specific product page or request an authorized description when identity, claims, or action are unclear. A website or app product must include an accessible link or supporting screenshots before visual development.

## Phase 1: Validate Sources and Assets

Build a source register before creative work:

- Record each source, role, authority, access status, usage caveat, and whether it is product identity, factual evidence, style, layout, or mood reference.
- Inspect every image, video, audio, and document for readability, dimensions, duration, format, corruption, duplicates, and actual product relevance.
- Separate verified facts, visible identity details, user claims, proposed copy, and unknowns. Do not turn a visible suggestion into a verified claim.
- Check packaging, logo, interface, people, and reference-media rights when relevant.
- Resolve conflicting names, labels, specifications, or brand cues by asking which source is authoritative.

Stop for the smallest missing input when product identity, factual basis, required asset, access, or delivery target cannot be established. Low-resolution or ambiguous media can guide non-factual direction but cannot prove exact labels, claims, or package details.

## Phase 2: Build Product Visuals and Confirm Creative Direction

Prepare a clean product visual before video production:

- Generate or derive a product-on-clean-background image when it improves packaging recognition, continuity, or later video references.
- Preserve the product shape, logo, label, color, texture, material, and visible text from the strongest approved source. Do not invent packaging copy or benefits.
- Treat the finalized artwork as an already established visual system. Reuse it; do not call the style unset merely because the user supplied images rather than style adjectives. Offer alternatives only for genuinely unresolved creative dimensions within that system. New settings, lighting or composition that depart from bound modules must be identified and approved before use.
- Ask whether the film should use a real person/model or remain product-only. If a model is chosen, request or prepare an authorized model reference and define identity continuity.

After the artwork gate and missing-input question card, confirm the product brief (15–30 seconds in five-second shots): title, type, model preference, name, category, positioning, audience, goal, up to three evidence-backed selling points, tone, CTA, language, duration, aspect ratio, channel, and audio route. Obtain explicit approval of the selected direction and compact pre-production package before moving to the storyboard.

## Phase 3: Create the Storyboard and Segment Plan

Write a full-duration Hook-Body-CTA script and storyboard. Every shot includes time, purpose, subject, action, composition, camera, transition, demonstrated fact or benefit, copy, readability hold, and audio intent. One shot sells one primary claim; the final shot holds product recognition and one CTA.

Build the shot list and approved audio plan using the production supplement. Identify hero versus supporting shots and explicitly state product absence when applicable. Batch-prepare reusable elements and an addressable visual reference for every shot; reuse already approved matching assets rather than regenerate them. Show the complete visual set and obtain approval before videos. A composite board may aid review, but every shot must remain individually addressable.

Plan 3–6 shots of 5 seconds each, for 15–30 seconds total. Specify source scene, narrative action, scale, angle, movement, purposeful hard cut, exact text, audio layers and brand holds. Define continuity locks for product, people, setting, light, copy and audio ownership. Do not repeat a shot merely to fill time.

### Match every video shot to finalized detail artwork

The finalized detail artwork is the shot-level authority for product composition, the demonstrated feature, explanatory copy and visual treatment, not merely an identity or color reference. Apply composition matching even to text-free video. An explicitly approved departure takes precedence only for the identified shot and property; do not infer a redesign from a request to animate.

1. **Bind sources before writing shots.** For every shot, identify the exact approved artwork/version and, for a long page, its module or region. Record what each source contributes: product identity, composition, demonstrated feature, copy or visual treatment. A detail-demonstration shot must use its corresponding detail module; an approved main image may support an opening or ending. Do not arbitrarily borrow another module's copy. A page may supply several shots and one shot may combine compatible sources, but their contributions and any approved departures must be explicit. Cover all user-required modules; selection or compression follows the approved scope, not a silent omission.
2. **Carry concrete composition and visible design.** Record the source product view/orientation, open or closed state, scale within the frame, placement, crop, key parts, relation to people/props, background structure, lighting character, negative space and visual reading order. Preserve the relevant product-to-text relationship, close-up/inset arrangement, arrows and label endpoints. Let the actual artwork carry palette and fine visual detail. Do not reduce a close-up detail module to an unrelated generic centered hero shot or replace its setting with a new studio template merely for convenience.
3. **Make the words describe the visible subject now.** Reuse accurate approved wording from the bound module, with its verified facts, units and qualifications. While a feature label is readable, its named part or use must be visible and unambiguous; keep leader lines attached to that part throughout motion. A mechanism explanation must show that mechanism, not another surface or a beauty shot. Do not attach one module's dimensions, component name or performance claim to another. A picture alone is not evidence of material composition or performance. Follow the existing typography rules for exact copy, font verification and approved substitutions; text-free requests remain text-free.
4. **Adapt to time and frame, not a new campaign.** In the full-duration storyboard, specify the source-matched composition during the main readable hold and the allowed entrance, exit, camera or product movement. For a new aspect ratio, preserve relative hierarchy, product recognizability, the named detail and product-to-copy association through proportionate reframing, spacing and line wrapping. Do not stretch the product, crop out the proof detail, recreate unsupported hidden structures or replace the source design with a generic overlay. If the requested motion or ratio genuinely conflicts with those anchors, show the specific adaptation for approval before production. Previously approved identical decisions are reused.
5. **Carry the mapping into actual production.** Each shot brief and production reference set must carry its bound artwork, concrete composition anchors, corresponding feature, exact approved copy and permitted motion. Attach the actual source images, not filenames or a prose summary alone. Additional clean product images and planning boards supplement, but never displace, the bound detail artwork; if reference limits prevent carrying required sources, resolve the route before generation. Ordinary references do not become exact opening or ending keyframes unless the user requests that timing. Separately composited text must still match the source design and the moving part it explains.
6. **Approve a complete correspondence table.** Include the table below within the existing storyboard review, not an extra duplicate approval round. Missing bindings, unreadable relevant source detail, conflicting versions or unresolved deviations block the affected shot. Request a readable replacement or the smallest needed decision; do not guess, silently drop a required module or use a generic substitute. A full source-to-shot mapping is required before video production, and all existing creative, artwork and storyboard gates remain.
7. **Verify shots against their own source.** Review each shot's representative composition/readable-hold frame beside its bound module, then play its motion and text timing. Check product state/view/scale/placement, visible feature, setting, composition, exact copy, typography, arrows, hierarchy, readable association and approved adaptations. A matching product with unrelated composition, or readable words describing an absent part, fails. Record per-shot pass/fail/unchecked with source version; repair only the failed shot or overlay and preserve accepted footage. Metadata and successful export do not prove visual matching; disclose unavailable review instead of passing it. This rule does not authorize regenerating existing media.

### Video typography must inherit the finalized artwork

Apply this rule whenever the approved promotional video needs on-screen advertising copy, specifications, labels or a CTA. It does not add text to a text-free video or authorize dialogue subtitles.

1. **Use the finished design as authority.** Before composing overlays, inspect the relevant finalized main/detail images and available approved copy or editable sources. Bind each text-bearing shot to a specific source artwork/version. Reuse its exact approved wording, language, numbers, units and qualifications. Select relevant existing text rather than copying the entire page. Verify uncertain letters or figures against the approved copy/source or ask; do not trust visual guessing. Shortening, translation, new facts or a new CTA require explicit approval in the storyboard. Do not resurrect unsupported claims merely because they appear in an image.
2. **Carry the whole typography system, not just the words.** Record headline/subheading/specification hierarchy, font family when verifiable, serif/sans character, weight, relative size, tracking, line spacing, line breaks, case, alignment, text position, margins, color relationships and any existing strokes, shadows, labels or background shapes. Let the source images carry their visual palette. Different shots may inherit different module roles, but they must form the same campaign system. Resolve genuinely conflicting approved styles with the user rather than mixing them arbitrarily.
3. **Adapt the layout to motion without redesigning it.** Retain source hierarchy, alignment, relative placement and whitespace; adapt size, wrapping and position only for the confirmed video frame, readable hold and subject movement. Keep text within safe areas and away from products, faces, paws and action paths. Use restrained entrance, hold and exit that fit the source design. Scene-attached or tracked lettering is used only when approved and technically feasible, not as a default effect. Do not replace the source treatment with generic white subtitles on a dark translucent rectangle, boxed parameter cards or decorative fonts unless that treatment belongs to the approved artwork or the user approves a change.
4. **Verify fonts and production capability.** Prefer a known, available and authorized original font or authorized supplied typography assets. A raster image alone does not prove the font name. If the exact font is unknown, unavailable or unsupported, disclose that limitation and request the font/source or approval of a visually matched substitute. Do not silently fall back to a system font or claim exact reproduction. If the current rendering route cannot reproduce the approved typography, resolve a supported composition route or an approved alternative before rendering; do not lower the design standard merely for convenience.
5. **Make it reviewable and render accurately.** The video storyboard records source artwork, exact copy, type hierarchy/style, placement, animation and readable timing for each overlay. Include typography in storyboard approval; a changed style needs a focused preview or review before final composition. Prefer clean footage with a separately composited, source-matched text layer when generated lettering cannot stay accurate. Do not paste a flattened whole poster over moving footage, duplicate baked-in text, or accept invented lettering. Reuse already approved style decisions without asking again.
6. **Compare the rendered result with the artwork.** Inspect representative video frames side by side with the source designs, and play the text entrance, hold and exit. Check wording, numbers, hierarchy, font appearance, spacing, alignment, palette, shapes, readability and moving-subject occlusion. Metadata or successful export alone cannot establish visual consistency. Repair only the affected overlay/shot; preserve valid footage. If verification is unavailable, say what remains unchecked. All existing artwork approval, Photoshop boundaries, opt-in subtitles and the 30-second total cap still apply; adding this rule does not authorize regenerating existing media.

## Phase 4: Check Model and Production Capability

Before high-cost generation, compare the approved storyboard, references, and segment plan with current capabilities:

- Verify duration, aspect ratio, resolution, reference count and media type, audio mode, and per-file or aggregate limits.
- Confirm the selected route can preserve product identity, readable packaging or interface text, people, motion, lip-sync, sound, and the planned shot structure.
- Decide whether segments should use one continuous reference route, multiple connected clips, native audio, separate speech/music, or deterministic post-production.
- If a hard requirement is unsupported, explain the gap and offer compatible alternatives; never silently change the requirement.
- Recheck whether every reference is appropriate for identity, style, layout, action, or mood, and use the strongest product source consistently.

Do not begin expensive generation while model, reference, duration, aspect, audio, or post-production dependencies remain unresolved.

## Phase 5: Produce Segments, Audio, and Final Assembly

Use the approved product visual, still-life images, storyboard, and continuity locks:

1. After the complete shot-visual set is approved, batch-produce a five-second video for each shot, carrying its step-3 reference, original bound artwork and correspondence table. Preserve shot-level composition and copy associations.
2. Retry only the affected segment when possible; simplify simultaneous actions before changing the product identity or claim.
3. Reuse the music choice confirmed before production. If a new audio conflict appears, pause before dependent work and ask only for that correction; do not postpone initial music intake until after segment generation.
4. Produce, import or reuse all approved storyboard audio layers after shot-video production; use the documented driving-audio dependency exception where required. Keep declined layers absent and one owner per layer.
5. Only after all required visuals and audio are ready, assemble the film with purposeful hard cuts, varied framing, exact source-matched text and authorized brand/packaging holds; check timing, aspect and sync, review and export or guide the actual export path.

When a segment already contains approved native audio, do not duplicate that layer. Do not present a segment, storyboard, still-life board, or preview as the final film.

## Phase 6: Review, Deliver, and State the Boundary

Review the final cut against the source register and approved package:

- Product identity, logo, packaging, interface, people, and reference-derived details remain faithful. Verify every shot against its bound artwork/version/module and approved correspondence table: product composition, visible feature, scene treatment and the exact copy-to-feature relationship must match or have an explicit approved adaptation.
- Claims and prices are supported, or proposed copy is clearly marked.
- Hook, body, CTA, segment order, total duration, aspect ratio, language, channel, and final-frame hold are correct; the measured export duration, including every title and ending, is no longer than 30 seconds.
- Text is readable and safe-area compliant; compare rendered typography against the bound finalized main/detail artwork for copy, font appearance, hierarchy, layout, spacing, palette and shapes, including motion and occlusion. A readable but unrelated generic overlay is not a pass. Visual continuity and transitions are acceptable.
- Audio is synchronized, balanced, and free of duplicate speech or music.
- Verify five-second shot allocations, 15–30-second total or an explicit timing exception, per-shot visual coverage, hero/supporting roles, purposeful cuts and required uninterrupted Logo/packaging holds. Verify the actual exported file, not only an edit timeline.

Deliver the final video by exact filename, with a concise creative summary, approved copy, source notes, and caveats. Separately identify product visuals, still-life images, storyboard, segments, previews, and source files only when requested.

## Failure Recovery

- **Source inaccessible or ambiguous**: request a specific product page, export, screenshot, or authorized description.
- **Asset fails inspection**: request a readable replacement or use it only for non-factual direction.
- **Product visual drift**: return to the strongest approved product source and regenerate only the affected visual or segment.
- **Direction rejected**: revise the selected direction and reconfirm before storyboard or production.
- **Storyboard gap**: repair timing and shot coverage before generating segments.
- **Model capability gap**: explain the unsupported requirement and offer compatible duration, framing, reference, audio, or post-production options.
- **Composition or explanation mismatch**: return to that shot's bound detail module; restore the intended view, visible part and text association. Preserve valid shots, and seek focused approval only if a new deviation is needed. Do not replace the source with a generic hero shot.
- **Shot or text failure**: simplify only the affected action or move exact source-matched typography to approved post-production. Any copy reduction or font/style substitution needs approval; do not replace the artwork design with a generic overlay.
- **Audio conflict**: choose generated BGM or user-provided audio, assign unique layer ownership, and rebuild the mix.
- **Partial segment failure**: preserve successful segments and retry only failed or missing segments.

## Boundaries

This Skill is for short, evidence-based promotional videos and their approved product visuals, still-life planning, storyboard, segments, and final assembly. It does not verify legal ownership, replace legal or regulatory review, invent product benefits, guarantee provider output, create long narrative films, perform unrelated general editing, or substitute a storyboard or preview for a requested finished video. Final acceptance remains with the user or brand owner.
