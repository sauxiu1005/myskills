# Continuous camera motion and action budgeting

Use for both video routes and runtime revisions, alongside spatial continuity. The route defines narrative/camera structure; rendering medium is separately approved.

## Route A: immersive FPV entry

Required chain: low travel → approach the principal person → close orbit → pull back into the artwork composition → freeze. Enter near the scene's ground or water surface using recognizable foreground passing points and a clear physical corridor, not slow zooming. The orbit needs lateral displacement around the subject, changing viewing surfaces and background occlusion/parallax; the subject turning, a stationary pan or push/pull cannot substitute. Plan natural subject action, gaze and expression during entry and orbit separately from simultaneous camera travel; the person cannot remain a static endpoint throughout. For unpeopled art, use a main object and appropriate environmental change without invented actors or anthropomorphism.

One take is possible, not compulsory. Once approved, failure does not authorize cuts. During pull-back the person naturally completes the painted pose while camera direction, relative scale, subject positions, objects and environmental framing converge. No teleporting, identity/background swap or sudden inserted original can stand in for arrival.

For an orbit, specify a world-space center, direction, scene-appropriate arc, radius, height, framing, duration and entry/exit states. Do not require a full circle or enter physically inaccessible space. Keep the orbit leg readable before adding a push-in or height change. Actor rotation and camera travel are independent. Foreground vegetation, midground terrain, and distant scenery should show different parallax; faces changing orientation alone do not establish an orbit. Crossing the action axis is motivated by visible continuous travel, not a mirrored set. Preserve routes clear of bodies, linked arms, props, and solid vegetation.

Plan the close-up as a continuation of the orbit's exit rather than a sudden viewpoint change. Motivate the next move through gaze or action, retain off-screen partners in the state ledger, and give a retreating camera room to yield to running subjects. Do not replace camera retreat with shrinking people or an unexplained zoom.

## Route B: a story leading up to the painting

Required chain: visible artwork/context → grounded or explicitly dramatized antecedent → subject goal/action → visible consequence → naturally form the painted moment → freeze. Start meaningfully apart from the endpoint. Motivated cuts with varied viewpoints and shot scales are mandatory; each shot reveals an event, detail, reaction or consequence rather than restaging a static pose. Do not apply FPV's optional one-take rule. Shot count follows story and duration, never a fixed template. Do not add people, locations, animals, conflict or stock actions merely for richness.

Keep key actions and results readable; a cut, splash or occlusion cannot hide an unperformed action. Carry action progress, body position, gaze, prop ownership, direction and environment into the next shot, without repeats or resets. The narrative consequence, not an unrelated appended still, creates the original composition.

## Performance and environment
For each shot write start state → goal/motivation → active action → support/contact/weight transfer → body/prop/environmental response → end state → next-shot inheritance. Describe camera and subject motion separately, with their temporal relationship. A released prop originates with its holder and follows a continuous trajectory; no unexplained hand swaps, copies or returns. Water, hair, cloth and ambient motion support action rather than replace it. Retain purposeful simple actions and remove overloaded repetitions, not all visible energy or normal speed.

Tie expression to events: noticing, deciding, approaching, reacting, relaxing. Let gaze, head, shoulders, weight transfer, and steps overlap naturally instead of alternating posed actions and pauses. Contacts require approach, support, and release. Give props a consistent holder; handoffs are receive-before-release. Preserve garment weight and planted feet.

Keep terrain, light direction, and wind stable in world space. Their screen directions can change during an orbit. Hair, vegetation, veils, and heavy cloth respond differently to the same wind. Unseen scenery is design completion, not evidence about the historical location.

## Natural material and style timing

Visual medium follows the approved project. Where a realistic opening transforms only at the end, distinguish skin, hair, fabric, water and plants. Avoid smoothed/waxy skin, uniform gloss, plastic texture, HDR-like sharpening and heavy paint smears before the approved transformation. Natural does not mean uniformly matte: retain plausible local reflections in wet cloth, water or reflective costume. The painting supplies appearance, clothing, world and color evidence, not a frozen pose or early paint texture. Hidden clothing and off-canvas space remain design completion. Keep each selected model in project state and verify current capabilities rather than prescribing a provider.

## Duration revisions
Reserve convergence, freeze, transformation and hold before allocating action; frozen transformation counts toward the static ending. Sustain meaningful progression rather than spending the latter half drifting, retreating or holding. Timing splits and shot counts are project choices, and precise intervals are targets rather than frame-accurate guarantees. List both action time and camera travel time; shortening a film is a new timing design, not accelerated playback or a long prompt pasted into a shorter duration. Protect the user's core action and camera move, shorten travel distances or repeated gestures first, and make any omitted story beats explicit. Do not quietly remove requested actions or change an approved orbit angle.

Check the implied speed: angle divided by time gives average angular speed, while radius determines travel distance. A full circle in a few seconds is visibly energetic, not an extremely slow orbit. If the requested actions cannot fit coherently, explain the concrete conflict and offer a longer runtime or a clearly identified simplification before rendering. A dense approved plan may be attempted, but do not promise every beat will be exact.

Track requested source duration, measured returned duration, and final assembled duration separately. Stable source handles may support editing, but build the final timeline from actual files; nominal clip sums do not establish the delivered duration. Durations, orbit angles and frame rates are project parameters. A failed take does not authorize cuts in an approved one-take plan.

## Assets and execution
Inspect whether existing identity references cover side/back views and whether the scene supports the camera route. Reuse sufficient character/scene assets; action text and spatial agreements handle motion. Missing action stills are not automatic blockers. Only unresolved critical geometry or a concrete input limitation justifies a minimal authorized supplement; never default to one image per action or shot. A portrait-format keyframe should not be silently cropped into a landscape starting frame; distinguish a design reference from a binding first frame. Query current model duration, ratio, reference limits, and continuation capabilities at execution time; do not hard-code a provider or capability table into this skill.

For split generation, inherit actual terminal pose, camera position, direction, velocity, props, lighting, and wind. A still last frame does not prove motion continuity; prefer video continuation where supported. Check the seam and describe a stitched result accurately.

## Acceptance

Use [evidence and acceptance](evidence-acceptance.md) for actual motion, sound, ending and conflicting reports. Inspect the orbit densely enough to establish continuous travel and parallax; isolated front/back frames cannot establish a complete orbit or exact angle. Inspect stitched continuation seams and label the method honestly.
