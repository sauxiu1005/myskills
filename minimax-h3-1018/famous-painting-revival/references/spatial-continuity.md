# Spatial relationships and blocking preflight (required before video)

Valid character, scene, and prop assets do not automatically form a physically workable shot. Before concrete production approval, create a compact spatial relationship card for each reusable location, then check every shot against it. Generic phrases such as 'same scene' or 'keep consistent' do not replace testable relationships.

1. **World-space anchors:** Define orientation using doors, windows, walls, and fixed furnishings, not permanent screen-left/right. Assign stable character and prop identifiers; record position zones, facing, scale, walkways, and mobility. A new shot scale or reverse angle must not mirror the room or rearrange people, chairs, or easels. Each distinct location has its own card.
2. **Function and reachability:** For every interaction record who stands on which side, faces what, operates which surface, uses which hand, and aims at which target. Check front/back/working surfaces, grip, support, reach, clear sightlines, and collision-free paths. In a painting studio, the painter stands on the painted-face side of the canvas; that face points toward the painter, and the brush contacts it rather than the stretcher back. The model remains visible to the painter. A camera may legitimately see the back of the canvas, but this does not authorize painting on that back. A sitter viewing the portrait must move to its visible-face side, or the easel must visibly turn; a cut must not magically flip the canvas.
3. **Shot-state ledger:** Record each character's start position and facing, prop position and visible/working side, gaze targets, movement paths, camera position and viewing side, end state, and what the next shot inherits. Fixed positions stay fixed. Motivated walking, yielding space, and handoffs are allowed, but require explicit causes, paths, and before/after states. Elliptical cuts must identify the time jump and permitted state changes; they cannot excuse unexplained spatial jumps.
4. **Camera axis:** Establish the primary character/prop action axis and stay on one side by default. Derive reverse-angle screen positions from world relationships instead of insisting that a painter is always screen-left. Establish a necessary axis crossing through a clear spatial shot or visible camera movement. Camera movement does not move characters or props.
5. **Preflight table:** Mark blocking, prop function/facing, reach, sightlines and adjacent-shot continuity as supported, conflicting or unknown, with the actual character/scene references and action/spatial reasoning. A missing action still is not a conflict or automatic blocker; static contact never proves dynamic success. Distinguish an unspecified but resolvable action from a critical physical contradiction or essential unknown. Resolve genuine critical blockers before the affected video, not every unseen movement by generating a picture.
6. **Resolution and approval:** First resolve relationships in action prose and spatial agreements using existing basic assets. Only a concrete technical limitation or otherwise unresolved critical spatial ambiguity justifies a minimal diagram or populated reference, with explicit authorization for additional assets. Include authorized assets in the complete B package, without per-image approval or repeated picture-repair loops. Report incompatible old geometry rather than silently flipping props or retaining it. After changes, update shot states, active bindings and downstream dependencies; preserve rejected/superseded versions for history but exclude them from execution.

Minimum spatial-card fields: scene identifier | fixed landmarks and orientation | character identifiers, positions and facing | prop identifiers, positions and working-surface orientation | camera zone/action axis | allowed movements and paths | protected invariants.


Basic asset roles: original artwork supplies creative evidence and endpoint; neutral front/side/back character views supply identity; reusable scene assets supply environment; video prompts supply action and cuts. Establish main-view authority where supporting views drift; multi-view sheets are not exact 3D geometry. Scene viewpoints and separate props are added only for actual coverage gaps. Apply FPV path geometry to route A and motivated cut continuity to route B; neither route freezes the people from the opening.
